# Human model

Describe enough of a person's current situation to help them act and correct the description.

A useful model separates:

- Intention: what the person wants now.
- Context: what is happening and what they already know.
- Preferences: how they want assistance presented.
- Constraints: what limits a feasible next action.
- Boundaries and permissions: what may be shared or done.
- Provenance: what the person said, when, and which parts are interpretations.

Keep long-term preferences separate from temporary states. A difficulty today does not define the person.

The owner can inspect, correct and revise the model, and decide what is shared. An agent's interpretation remains labeled until the owner reviews it.

Start with the [alignment model](HUMAN_ALIGNMENT.md) and its synthetic example. The model represents a person for a purpose; it does not exhaust the person.
